Route planner

Routes inside Gale Crater follow the path Curiosity actually drove, so the ground is proven passable. Other routes are drawn as the shortest path over the surface.

Speeds are planning assumptions you can change. The turn-back rule (leave 10:30, back by 15:30 local solar time) comes from our temperature layer.

Three ways to travel

1. On foot (default 2 km/h): short walks near base, in pairs, inside the GO window.
2. Pressurised rover (default 10 km/h, our planning figure): longer trips with a shirt-sleeve cabin and a place to shelter.
3. Nuclear-Powered Navigator (concept aircraft, default 350 km/h): flies about 1.5 km above the ground and lands upright, so it can reach anywhere on Mars in hours.
Navigator figures come from NASA-funded studies: the nuclear-powered MANGLE concept (cruise about Mach 0.4, roughly 350 km/h, at 1.5 km, range enough to circle Mars 160 times in a Mars year; AIAA 2014-3820) and the ARES Mars airplane (designed cruise 145 m/s, about 520 km/h). Thin air and a low speed of sound limit how fast any Mars aircraft can go. For scale, the Ingenuity helicopter's record was 10 m/s (36 km/h) at up to 24 m. No crewed Mars aircraft exists yet.
